In Loving Memory of Eleanor Vance

March 14, 1948 — November 22, 2025 · Service: St. Luke's Memorial Chapel

A devoted teacher of 38 years, master botanist, and beloved matriarch whose radiant wisdom, generous kitchen, and unforgettable bedtime stories touched generations of family and community members.

Words from Spouse & Children
🤝 Help & Sacrifice
“What's something she did every day that was a quiet act of devotion?”

Every morning at 6:15 AM, before anyone else was awake, Eleanor would brew fresh chamomile tea and leave a handwritten quote on the kitchen island for me and the kids. Over 52 years of marriage, that meant nearly 19,000 notes of steady, quiet encouragement.

— Robert Vance (Spouse)
✨ Joy & Laughter
“What's a moment she made you proud in a way that took your breath away?”

When she was 71, she found out the local elementary school was shutting down its music program. Without asking for anyone's permission or praise, she gathered 8 retired teachers, petitioned the town board, and organized after-school string classes from our living room for 4 years.

— Claire Vance-Miller (Daughter)
Memories from Childhood & Lifelong Friends
👁️ Witnessing in Action
“What was an adventure or secret from growing up that only the two of you knew about?”

In the summer of 1962, we built a raft out of discarded cedar fence posts and floated all the way down to Mill Creek. When it capsized in the mud, Eleanor didn't cry—she burst into uncontrollable laughter and said, 'Now we know how to build a better one.' That was her entire philosophy on life.

— Martha Hayes (Childhood Friend)
Mentees, Neighbors & Admirers
🛡️ Pain & Resilience
“What was a specific moment they believed in your potential before you could see it yourself?”

When I failed my student teaching evaluation in 1994 and was ready to give up on education entirely, Mrs. Vance sat with me for three hours after school, rebuilt my lesson plans with me, and told me: 'You are going to change children's lives. Don't you dare let one bad day tell you who you are.' I have been teaching for 30 years because of her.

— Sarah Jenkins (Former Student & Mentee)
